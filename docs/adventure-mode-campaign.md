# Phase 7: the Warden's trail

The campaign is an experimental, complete run using the same `step()` rules in
the browser and command-line simulator. Turn on **Show adventure mode** in
Settings, press **Start adventure**, choose Ranger or Sentinel on the campaign selection screen, and start a run. The earlier five simulator
loadouts remain explicitly labeled sandbox probes and continue their endless
wolf experiments; they are not campaign starters.

## Playing a run

Choose a reachable location on the route map. The map moves forward through 15
columns, with two or three lanes crossing and merging into one boss. A fight's
type and danger tier are public; specific enemies appear on arrival, and random gear is rolled at the kill. Unknown locations conceal their category until reached. The first shop
is guaranteed at column 4. There are two forage columns; neither can be revisited.
The route contains eight fights including the boss, two shops, two rest/event
choices, one event, and two forage puzzles. Quiet stops take no puzzle.

**Next puzzle** enters the current encounter. Each puzzle is 9×9 medium, three
acorns, no freebies, and unrestricted What if?. Leaving for the menu or closing
the browser preserves the puzzle. Regular play keeps its own save and statistics.
A new puzzle does not let the enemy attack immediately: its existing countdown
continues, and the player's next reveal still resolves first. Wrong guesses and
hint reveals retain their phase 1 turn rules.

Tap an equipment chip to inspect its color swatches and exact size bands,
charge/cooldown, level, and free readiness control. Tap the enemy for exact health,
next damage and countdown, enrage/escape, and harvest colors. These cards also
expose instant consumables. All have keyboard-accessible controls and Escape
closes the card. Harvest colors are visible on the strip before the kill. Tap an equipped item or skill to toggle readiness; hold it for half a second to inspect. This works in the puzzle strip and on equipment cards. Keyboard users can press Enter/Space to toggle and Shift+Enter for details.

The home screen contains only **Start adventure** and **Resume adventure** for this mode. Selection and run management use the shared Phaser canvas. **Route**, **Gear**, **Loot**, **Camp/Shop**, and **Potions** have separate views with touch and keyboard controls; inspections and equipment swaps have their own detail views. Returning from a puzzle opens run management, and **Home** returns to ordinary play. Starting selection does not replace a saved run until confirmed. Both puzzle clocks pause in these views, and reloading restores preparation or the open puzzle.

Between completed puzzles, including unfinished fights, gear can move freely
between four ordered equipment slots and three pack slots. Swaps move the whole
item state; they do not reset anything. Normal round boundaries clear block,
cooldowns, and ordinary charges on packed and equipped items. Readiness persists.
The menu only offers route movement, loot, shopping, or resting once the current
fight is over. A normal enemy can be fled between puzzles: its current hit lands,
block can absorb it, and it grants no loot. The boss cannot be fled.

## Starting gear and provisional content

Palette colors below are numbered 1–9 as in the inspection cards. Values are for
1–3 / 4–7 / 8–14 / 15+ squares.

| Herbalist, victory unlock | 22 | Thorn, colors 6–7, attack 2/3/4/5 | Remedy, color 8, heal 2/3/5/7 | 3 |

The small loot catalog covers immediate damage, block, healing, a cooldown
weapon, a charge weapon, and supplies. Rolls independently shuffle each item's
colors. There is no guaranteed improvement or lack of overlap. Gear tiers add
one to every band per tier above the first. Elite locations have higher danger,
health, reward tiers and three harvest colors rather than two.

Each kill rolls **2–4 gear items plus one potion** (elites and the boss guarantee at least three gear items), then grants 9/12/15 supplies
by tier. The independent per-node random stream makes offers stable after reload
and pairs each location's drops across policies, regardless of earlier routes.
Rewards are generated by the kill command and taken only after completing the
puzzle. Items that do not fit can replace an equipped item or be left behind;
travel discards pending loot. No retries, revisits, or stock rerolls exist.

Normal enemies have 8–14 health before elite bonuses. Raiders escape after three
puzzles with their loot; wolves and ogres add six damage to each hit for each
additional puzzle they survive. The Warden has 48 health and adds eight damage
per extra puzzle. Escalation eventually exceeds bounded healing and block, so
stalling cannot produce an unlimited safe economy. Attack countdowns are never
reset by escalation, swaps, or navigation decisions.

## Recovery, spending, and harvest

There is no automatic postcombat heal. A rest restores one third of maximum
health, rounded up, **or** improves one equipped item. Events offer a calm exit,
six supplies for six health, or four damage for a random gear item; taking a
lethal risk ends the run. These resolve once, without a puzzle.

A shop has three fixed gear offers (25/35/45 supplies by tier), three potions
(10 for healing, 15 for block/damage), and healing at two supplies per health.
Sell packed items for half their original gear value. Consumables occupy three
separate slots: restore nine health, gain ten block, or deal ten enemy damage.
They cost no tactical turn, work mid-puzzle and between puzzles, and cannot
resurrect a dead character. A between-puzzle damage-potion kill still receives
one final puzzle for harvest rather than an unlimited series of safe puzzles.

After a kill, a matching readied item takes a color as experience; healing gear
continues to heal. Every 24 squares improves the band's values by one step
(the highest band grows by two), at most once per item per fight. Experience
persists; levels cap at three. Rest improvement uses the same upgrade and cap.
Unreadying gear lets an unclaimed harvest color become 1/2/3 supplies by size;
each already-completed combat puzzle subtracts one from that yield, down to
zero. Other colors fizzle. Forage locations instead harvest every color for one
puzzle, regardless of equipment, at 1/2 supplies by size. Each source is finite.

Boss victory locks on its killing reveal. The rest of the puzzle still counts
for healing, harvest and upgrades; then the terminal summary appears. Losing or
abandoning that final harvest puzzle preserves the secured victory. Earlier
puzzle loss, character death, or confirmed abandonment is a defeat. Replacing an
unfinished run records abandonment before archiving it. Victory unlocks Herbalist
in a separate storage key, surviving deaths and the ten-run archive rolling over.

## Saves and reports

Campaign state, puzzle, accepted commands, and rewards are saved in one adventure
write. The unlock key is separate; a saved victory repairs a missing unlock after
reload. The existing storage prefixes and version-1 sandbox saves remain valid.

Each puzzle record preserves its seed code, decoded generator settings, explicit
color mapping, active solve time, hints, reveal sources, mistakes and What if?
entries. App/content versions are retained per puzzle, including mixed-version
observations. This does not claim to infer planning time from wall-clock gaps.

Active campaigns follow current rules. When `CONTENT_VERSION` changes, current
catalog effects and enemy budgets refresh while random colors, instance ids,
levels, item timers, enemy damage already taken, and attack countdowns survive.
Unknown/removed item ids retain their saved definition as a recovery fallback.
Ended campaigns and legacy sandbox definitions are retained for historical
comparisons. Mixed-version logs need the recorded version context; they are not
claimed to replay historical rules under a newer executable.

Run `pnpm campaign --seeds 10 --generated 30 --rounds 30` to write the report and
raw metrics to `reports/phase7/` (gitignored). Positional CSV files select source
datasets; `--seed`, `--seeds`, `--generated`, `--rounds`, `--generated-only`, and `--out` are supported.
Original recordings are checked against rebuilt boards. Eligible recordings
supply medium board geometry without silver mode or freebies; their campaign
reveal sequences are explicitly modeled, not exact player replays. Generated
boards supply an independent comparison pool. Neither repeats a board within a
trial; a short pool yields an unresolved result rather than padding it.

Every actual starter runs on matched seeds with coverage, weak-color, and
recovery gear preferences, under ordinary solver reveals and deliberate
planning, with zero or one labeled synthetic mistake per puzzle. Planning banks
all solver deductions without reading the hidden solution, then searches reveal
and readiness sequences with a three-turn horizon and beam width four. This is
a reproducible bounded policy, not an optimum or evidence of human performance.

Reports separate victory, being alive, fights won, escapes, unresolved runs,
health after each puzzle, first useful gear, first encounter completion, gear,
upgrades, income and spending. Raw JSON retains starting definitions, full final
states, replayable commands, codes, dataset fingerprints and source identifiers.
The lowest tenth of *observed* gear-color widths is shown separately; this
conditional metric is a small descriptive proxy for loot luck, not a guarantee
about all possible drop sequences or an independent causal comparison.

The target remains roughly 12–18 puzzles and 30–45 minutes, with individual
fights supplying most variation. Independent human playtesting of onboarding,
route readability, planning, pacing, and economy remains necessary. Final
balance, victory-rate targets, additional starters, and finished art belong to
phase 8. Automated completion is evidence that the loop works, not that these
provisional values are balanced.

## Integrated browser completion (2026-11-03)

An automated browser playthrough with the actual Sentinel starter completed the
interactive campaign: 12 distinct generated puzzles, eight fights won, and
27/28 health at victory. It used banked solver deductions and the same bounded
planner, collected and swapped gear through the controller, visited shops and
quiet locations, and used the scene's normal reveal path. Only finish-banner
animation waits were skipped; no combat values or saved state were changed.

The exported 254-command journal replays exactly through the pure engine, with
no rejected commands, and retains all 12 puzzle reconstruction records. The run
has 108 reveals, 208 supplies earned, 25 spent, 11 acquired items and four upgrades.
Its simple shop policy bought recovery rather than testing an optimal economy;
the unused supplies are a tuning question, not evidence that prices are right.
This validates integration and terminal rewards, not independent human onboarding,
planning time, or a population victory rate.
